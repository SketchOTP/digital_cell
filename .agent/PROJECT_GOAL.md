# End Goal Statement

Create a standalone Linux-based digital lifeform that begins as a single self-maintaining digital protocell and develops into a persistent, embodied creature whose continued existence arises from its own internal organization rather than scripted behavior.

The organism must be composed of interacting digital chemistry and cellular structures that:

* consume energy and material from their environment
* continuously rebuild and regulate themselves
* maintain a functional boundary between self and environment
* grow, repair damage, adapt, reproduce, inherit variation, and die
* reorganize structurally through experience
* develop sensory, motor, memory, and regulatory systems from simpler foundations
* generate behavior through internal dynamics, learned predictions, developmental history, and environmental interaction
* form an individual identity that cannot be reduced to predefined personality values, reaction tables, animation states, or an LLM prompt

The mature organism will inhabit a persistent digital environment presented as though the computer screen were glass separating its world from ours. It will perceive activity beyond that boundary through a webcam and microphone, gradually learn recurring people, sounds, patterns, and relationships, and respond through movement, posture, proximity, environmental manipulation, and nonverbal vocalization.

Its behavior must remain autonomous when no person is present. It must explore, rest, maintain itself, alter its environment, consolidate experience, form habits, develop preferences, and continue changing over time without waiting for user input.

No central controller may directly command the organism to eat, repair, approach, bond, become afraid, reproduce, or perform a scripted emotional response. These outcomes must emerge from the interactions among its digital cells, metabolism, body, nervous system, memories, environment, and developmental processes.

The final system will be considered successful when:

1. Its body and internal organization are continuously produced and maintained by its own lower-level processes.
2. Its continued existence depends on real simulated resource and energy flows.
3. Damage, starvation, development, reproduction, and death occur causally rather than through scripted state changes.
4. Different life histories produce observably different individuals.
5. Past experiences permanently alter future perception, structure, behavior, and relationships.
6. It independently develops coherent behaviors and rituals that were not explicitly authored.
7. Its actions are understandable from its condition and history but are not mechanically predictable.
8. It continues living and developing when unobserved.
9. Removing essential biological organization or accumulated history causes measurable loss of identity and capability.
10. An observer can reasonably perceive it as a persistent living creature rather than an animated application, virtual pet, chatbot, or game character.

The project does not claim to prove consciousness. Its goal is to create the strongest technically defensible form of digital life possible: a self-producing, self-maintaining, developing, evolving, embodied individual whose apparent aliveness comes from what it is and how it continues to exist—not from how convincingly it has been scripted to perform life.
